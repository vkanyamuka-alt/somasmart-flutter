## Aim

To verify Ohm's law and find the resistance of a resistor.

## Theory

**Ohm's law:** the current through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it, provided the temperature and other physical conditions stay constant.

$$
V \propto I \quad\Rightarrow\quad V = IR
$$

$R$ is the resistance in ohms (Ω). A graph of $V$ against $I$ is a straight line through the origin and its gradient is the resistance:

$$
R = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta I}
$$

An **ammeter** measures current and is connected in **series** with the resistor. A **voltmeter** measures potential difference and is connected in **parallel** across the resistor.

## Apparatus

- Power supply (0–6 V), switch
- Ammeter, voltmeter
- 10 Ω resistor, connecting wires

## Procedure

1. Connect the power supply, switch, ammeter and resistor in series. Connect the voltmeter across the resistor.
2. Close the switch and set the supply to 1 V. Record the voltmeter and ammeter readings.
3. Increase the voltage in steps up to 6 V, recording $V$ and $I$ each time.
4. Open the switch between readings so the resistor does not heat up.
5. Plot $V$ against $I$ and calculate the gradient.

## Precautions

- Connect the positive terminals of the meters towards the positive of the supply.
- Read the meters straight on to avoid parallax.
- Keep currents small so the temperature of the resistor stays constant.
