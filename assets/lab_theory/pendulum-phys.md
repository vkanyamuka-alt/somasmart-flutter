## Aim

To determine the acceleration due to gravity, $g$, using a simple pendulum.

## Theory

A simple pendulum is a small heavy bob on a light, inextensible string. For small swings (less than about 10°) the time for one complete oscillation, the **period** $T$, depends only on the length $l$ and on $g$:

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}
$$

Squaring both sides gives

$$
T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{g}\, l
$$

so a graph of $T^2$ against $l$ is a straight line through the origin with gradient $\dfrac{4\pi^2}{g}$. Therefore

$$
g = \frac{4\pi^2}{\text{gradient}}
$$

The length $l$ is measured from the point of suspension to the **centre** of the bob.

## Apparatus

- Retort stand and clamp, split cork
- Thread and pendulum bob
- Metre rule, stopwatch

## Procedure

1. Clamp the thread between the two halves of the cork and set the length to 0.30 m.
2. Pull the bob aside through a small angle and release it so it swings in one plane.
3. Time 20 complete oscillations; repeat and take the average. $T = \dfrac{t}{20}$.
4. Repeat for at least five lengths, for example 0.30 m to 1.00 m.
5. Tabulate $l$, $t$, $T$ and $T^2$, plot $T^2$ against $l$ and find the gradient.

## Precautions

- Keep the angle of swing small.
- Start timing as the bob passes the centre of its swing, where it moves fastest.
- Time many oscillations to reduce the effect of your reaction time.
