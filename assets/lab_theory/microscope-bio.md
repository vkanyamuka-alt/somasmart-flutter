## Aim

To prepare a temporary slide of onion epidermis, stain it and observe plant cells under the light microscope.

## Theory

The light microscope uses two lenses to magnify a thin specimen. The total magnification is

$$
\text{magnification} = \text{eyepiece magnification} \times \text{objective magnification}
$$

so a ×10 eyepiece with a ×40 objective magnifies 400 times.

Plant cells are mostly transparent. **Iodine solution** stains the nucleus and cytoplasm light brown (and starch blue-black), so the parts of the cell are easier to see.

Each onion epidermal cell shows:

- a **cell wall** of cellulose, giving a regular, brick-like shape
- a thin layer of **cytoplasm** lining the wall
- a **nucleus**, usually near the edge of the cell
- a large central **vacuole**

There are no chloroplasts, because the onion bulb grows underground and does not photosynthesise.

## Apparatus and materials

- Light microscope, glass slide, cover slip
- Onion bulb, forceps, mounted needle, scalpel
- Iodine solution, dropper, filter paper

## Procedure

1. Peel a thin piece of epidermis from the inside of an onion scale leaf and lay it flat in a drop of water on the slide.
2. Lower a cover slip at an angle with the mounted needle so no air bubbles are trapped.
3. Add a drop of iodine at one edge of the cover slip and draw it under with filter paper.
4. Start with the low-power objective and focus with the coarse adjustment, then switch to high power and use only the fine adjustment.
5. Draw a few cells, label them and state the magnification.

## Precautions

- Never focus downwards with the coarse adjustment on high power; you may crack the slide.
- Use a thin, single layer of cells so light can pass through.
- Make drawings with clear, continuous lines, without shading.
