## Aim

To investigate osmosis in potato tissue and estimate the concentration of the cell sap.

## Theory

**Osmosis** is the movement of water molecules from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane.

When potato tissue is placed in a solution:

- **more dilute** than its cell sap, the cells take in water and the tissue **gains** mass;
- **more concentrated** than its cell sap, the cells lose water and the tissue **loses** mass;
- **equal in concentration** (isotonic), there is no net movement and the mass does not change.

The change in mass is worked out as a percentage, so cylinders of slightly different starting mass can be compared:

$$
\%\ \text{change in mass} = \frac{m_1 - m_0}{m_0} \times 100
$$

where $m_0$ is the starting mass and $m_1$ the final mass. On a graph of percentage change in mass against sucrose concentration, the point where the line crosses **0 %** gives the concentration of the cell sap.

## Apparatus and materials

- Potato, cork borer, scalpel, ruler, white tile
- Boiling tubes, sucrose solutions (0.0 to 1.0 M), labels
- Electronic balance, filter paper, stopwatch

## Procedure

1. Cut five potato cylinders of the same diameter and length and remove any skin.
2. Blot each cylinder dry, weigh it and record $m_0$.
3. Place one cylinder in each labelled sucrose solution for the same time.
4. Remove the cylinders, blot off surface solution and weigh again to get $m_1$.
5. Calculate the percentage change in mass and plot it against concentration.

## Precautions

- Cut all cylinders from the same potato so the cell sap is the same.
- Blot every cylinder in the same way before weighing.
- Leave all cylinders in their solutions for the same length of time.
