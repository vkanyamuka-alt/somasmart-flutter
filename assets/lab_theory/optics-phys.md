## Aim

To trace the path of a ray of light through a triangular glass prism and determine the refractive index of the glass.

## Theory

When light passes from air into glass it slows down and bends **towards** the normal. When it leaves the glass it bends **away** from the normal. **Snell's law** relates the angle of incidence $i$ and the angle of refraction $r$:

$$
n = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r}
$$

where $n$ is the refractive index of the glass. A graph of $\sin i$ against $\sin r$ is a straight line through the origin with gradient $n$.

For a prism of refracting angle $A$, the ray is deviated by the angle

$$
D = i + e - A
$$

where $e$ is the angle of emergence. If $i$ is too small, the ray meets the second face at more than the critical angle and is totally internally reflected, so no ray emerges.

## Apparatus

- Triangular glass prism (60°), drawing board, white paper
- Optical pins, protractor, ruler, sharp pencil

## Procedure

1. Trace the outline of the prism on the paper and draw a normal to the first face.
2. Draw an incident ray at angle $i$ and fix pins P₁ and P₂ on it.
3. Looking through the other face, fix P₃ and P₄ in line with the images of P₁ and P₂.
4. Remove the prism, join the points and measure $r$ and $e$.
5. Repeat for $i$ = 30°, 35°, 40°, 45°, 50° and 55°. Tabulate $i$, $r$, $e$, $D$, $\sin i$ and $\sin r$.
6. Plot $\sin i$ against $\sin r$; the gradient is $n$.

## Precautions

- Keep the pins upright and well apart.
- Look at the bottoms of the pins when lining up their images.
- Use a sharp pencil and keep the prism exactly on its outline.
