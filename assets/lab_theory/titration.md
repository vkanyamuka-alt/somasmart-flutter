## Aim

To find the concentration of a hydrochloric acid solution by titrating it with standard 0.10 M sodium hydroxide.

## Theory

An acid and an alkali neutralise each other to form a salt and water:

$$
\mathrm{HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) \longrightarrow NaCl(aq) + H_2O(l)}
$$

One mole of acid reacts with one mole of alkali. At the **end point** the moles of alkali added from the burette equal the moles of acid in the flask, so

$$
M_a V_a = M_b V_b
$$

where $M$ is the concentration in mol/dm³ and $V$ the volume. For a reaction that is not 1 : 1, use $\dfrac{M_a V_a}{M_b V_b} = \dfrac{n_a}{n_b}$, the mole ratio from the equation.

**Phenolphthalein** is colourless in acid and pink in alkali. The end point is the first drop of alkali that turns the whole solution a pale pink that stays when the flask is swirled.

## Apparatus and reagents

- 50 cm³ burette, 25.0 cm³ pipette with filler
- 250 cm³ conical flask, white tile, retort stand and clamp, funnel
- 0.10 M sodium hydroxide, hydrochloric acid of unknown concentration
- Phenolphthalein indicator

## Procedure

1. Rinse the burette with the alkali, fill it and read the initial volume at eye level, at the bottom of the meniscus.
2. Pipette 25.0 cm³ of the acid into the conical flask and add 2–3 drops of phenolphthalein.
3. Run in the alkali while swirling. Add it drop by drop near the end point.
4. Record the final reading. Repeat until two titres agree within 0.10 cm³ and average them.

## Calculation

$$
M_a = \frac{M_b V_b}{V_a} = \frac{0.10 \times \text{average titre}}{25.0}
$$

## Precautions

- Remove the funnel before titrating and make sure no air bubble is left below the tap.
- Read volumes to two decimal places (for example 23.40 cm³).
- Use the white tile so the colour change is easy to see.
