## Aim

To investigate how the concentration of sodium thiosulfate affects the rate of its reaction with hydrochloric acid.

## Theory

Sodium thiosulfate reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to form a fine yellow precipitate of sulfur:

$$
\mathrm{Na_2S_2O_3(aq) + 2HCl(aq) \longrightarrow 2NaCl(aq) + SO_2(g) + S(s) + H_2O(l)}
$$

The sulfur makes the mixture cloudy. The reaction is timed from adding the acid until a cross drawn under the flask can no longer be seen. The same amount of sulfur is needed to hide the cross each time, so

$$
\text{rate} \propto \frac{1}{t}
$$

According to the **collision theory**, a more concentrated solution has more particles in the same volume, so they collide more often and the reaction is faster. A graph of $\dfrac{1}{t}$ against concentration is a straight line through the origin.

## Apparatus and reagents

- Conical flasks, measuring cylinders, stopwatch
- White paper marked with a cross
- Sodium thiosulfate solution, dilute hydrochloric acid, distilled water

## Procedure

1. Put 50 cm³ of sodium thiosulfate solution in a flask and stand it on the cross.
2. Add 5 cm³ of hydrochloric acid, start the stopwatch and swirl once.
3. Stop the watch when the cross disappears and record the time $t$.
4. Repeat with the thiosulfate diluted with water, keeping the total volume at 50 cm³ (for example 40 + 10, 30 + 20, 20 + 30, 10 + 40).
5. Tabulate the volumes, $t$ and $\dfrac{1}{t}$, then plot $\dfrac{1}{t}$ against the volume (or concentration) of thiosulfate.

## Precautions

- Keep the total volume, the volume of acid and the temperature the same in every run.
- Look down at the cross from the same height each time.
- Do the reaction in a well-ventilated room; sulfur dioxide is irritating.
