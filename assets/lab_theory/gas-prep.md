## Aim

To prepare oxygen, hydrogen and carbon dioxide in the laboratory, collect them and identify each gas by its test.

## Theory

**Oxygen** is made by decomposing hydrogen peroxide. Manganese(IV) oxide is a catalyst: it speeds up the reaction and is unchanged at the end.

$$
\mathrm{2H_2O_2(aq) \xrightarrow{MnO_2} 2H_2O(l) + O_2(g)}
$$

**Hydrogen** is made by reacting zinc granules with dilute hydrochloric acid:

$$
\mathrm{Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) \longrightarrow ZnCl_2(aq) + H_2(g)}
$$

**Carbon dioxide** is made by reacting marble chips (calcium carbonate) with dilute hydrochloric acid:

$$
\mathrm{CaCO_3(s) + 2HCl(aq) \longrightarrow CaCl_2(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)}
$$

Oxygen and hydrogen are only slightly soluble in water, so they are collected **over water**. Carbon dioxide is denser than air and fairly soluble, so it is collected by **downward delivery**.

| Gas | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | Glowing splint | Relights |
| Hydrogen | Burning splint | Burns with a "pop" |
| Carbon dioxide | Bubble through limewater | Limewater turns milky |

## Apparatus

- Flat-bottomed flask, thistle funnel or dropping funnel, delivery tube
- Trough, beehive shelf, gas jars with covers
- Wooden splints, limewater

## Procedure

1. Set up the flask with the solid, the funnel and the delivery tube.
2. Add the liquid through the funnel, keeping its end below the liquid surface.
3. Let the first bubbles escape (they are mostly air), then collect the gas.
4. Cover each full jar and carry out the test for the gas.

## Precautions

- Hydrogen forms an explosive mixture with air: keep flames away from the apparatus.
- The end of the thistle funnel must dip into the liquid so the gas cannot escape.
- Remove the delivery tube from the water before stopping any heating, to prevent suck-back.
